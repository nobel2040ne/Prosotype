What it is

What a caption carries beyond the words.

A conventional subtitle preserves the words and discards the rest of the signal. Caption with Intention, published by the Chicago Hearing Society, restores three of the discarded properties: speaker identity as colour, utterance timing as motion, and vocal delivery as typography. Prosotype derives all three live, from a microphone, in English or Korean, without transmitting audio.

The pipeline

From capture to glyph, entirely on the local machine.

Language is chosen before capture and locked for the session, so a recognizer is never swapped under retained decoder state. Everything runs on the local CPU; the only network access the project permits is a one-time model download and the LAN link to the hardware node.

ConstraintReason
Gain is applied to the recognizer's copy only Prosody measures loudness from the captured signal. Applying gain before that measurement would map a whisper and a shout to the same type size.
Word onsets are never synthesised Prosody, the motion clock and the read-ahead floor are all keyed to per-word start/end. A recognizer that returns no timestamps can therefore supply text at the verifier boundary and nowhere else.
Utterances are reconciled, not rebuilt A structural mismatch between the streaming and verified transcripts occurs on nearly every utterance. Rebuilding the block would re-render the sentence at each pause; instead, matches are corrected in place, deletions dropped and insertions added at their spoken position.

The playhead

What makes this a CWI renderer rather than a subtitle track.

CWI needs the line on screen before it is spoken, and a live recogniser cannot produce text early. So the captions present from a clock that runs behind the acoustic one. Nothing is predicted — the delay is the lag, one for one.

RuleFigureWhy it is not the obvious thing
The playhead trails the acoustic clock 1.75 s Colouring only up to now − delay leaves real, recognised, still-uncoloured text ahead of the colour.
Read-ahead is a per-word floor 420 ms The recogniser releases words in bursts, so a delay moves the mean lead and leaves its spread alone: at 1.75 s, 42% of words still turned within 100 ms of appearing. The floor takes that to 0%.
The turn is never gated on the speaker 0.62 s vs 4.48 s Text arrives at a median 0.62 s and durable attribution at 4.48 s — so the turn happens on time in whatever colour is known, or not on time at all.

The caption invariant is structural. A word's colour turn is a fixed moment on the acoustic timeline, so text may be revised only while the word is still ahead of the playhead. Behind it is frozen history — spelling included. Measured, coloured-caption rewrites went 14 → 0.

The motion channels

What a word does when it is spoken.

Each channel has one owner and one input; they do not share state, and the browser schedules all of them from a single animation-delay, with no timer and no reveal queue. The design system names five. The shipped renderer runs seven, and departs from the specified values in three places — the table records both.

as each word is spoken

Colour turn. The word is presented in read-ahead ink and changes to its speaker colour at the spoken onset, wiped across its characters rather than switched. The keyframe declares no end state, so a later speaker correction applies as a direct write.

every word pops the same

Pop. The specified cue: a constant 15% on every word, growing from the baseline with no vertical translation. The shipped renderer halves it to 7.6% and applies it only to emphasised words.

softersofter oror louderlouder

Crest. Loudness is applied as a font-size rather than a transform, so the letterforms are genuinely set at that size. The row is unaffected: a hidden sizer holds the layout at resting size and the growing ink is overlaid on the row's own baseline.

a high voice a low one

Weight. A property of the voice rather than of the word. Read per word, the mapping inverts under emphasis — a shout roughly doubles F0, which would render the loudest delivery as the lightest type — so the register term reads the speaker's running median F0.

as each word is spoken

Hold lift. A word surrounded by silence rises and lands on its colour turn. The signal is isolation — the shorter of the gaps either side, over inter-onset intervals — not sustain: the recognizer cannot distinguish a drawn-out word from a word followed by a pause.

animation

Character wave. Texture beneath the colour turn, advancing one syllable at a time — se+en, but+ton. The grouping is measured from the reference film, in which adjacent glyphs step 42 ms apart with four elevated simultaneously.

The studio

Two design systems, and the boundary is the word.

The chrome follows the Apple design analysis; the captions follow CWI, which outranks it. The line is literal: anything inside a caption word is CWI, everything else is Apple. "UI recedes so the product can speak" is why the captions carry no frame and no per-block label — here the captions are the product. This page is built the same way, which is why every caption on it sits in a black box of its own.

Measured readings are not controls, so −22.4 dBFS carries no border, fill or pill. Speaker colour is the only colour on screen that encodes information, which is why the surrounding interface is achromatic. The bracketed sound label is placed inside the dial rather than at the foot of the stage: a non-speech event has a direction, and the dial is where direction is displayed. This is a placement deviation from the design system, not a removal.

What it can't do

Known limitations.

A microphone cannot know future words. The playhead buys the read-ahead back, but it buys it with 1.75 s of latency. Attribution is uncertain at the edges — short turns and similar voices stay ambiguous, and about half of all words first paint in a colour they will not keep; the UI shows that rather than guessing. English and Korean each keep their own model and their own numbers, chosen before capture. The haptic module has never run against real motors.

Every figure above is measured rather than asserted, and each measurement carries a negative control that is run first: a check never observed to fail is not evidence. The recurring failure modes have been a probe incapable of failing, a per-word average that normalised away the quantity under test, and numeric agreement accepted in place of visual inspection.
